1

Edition and research environment

Laertius is a digital scholarly edition of Diogenes Laertius designed for philological reading, structured investigation, and the critical reuse of scholarly data.

The environment brings together the Greek text, translation, passage-level navigation, prosopographical and geographical entities, philosophical traditions, and machine-readable assertions. Its interfaces support movement between close reading and computational enquiry while retaining the textual evidence on which interpretation depends.

2

Scholarly method

The edition treats digital representation as an explicit scholarly argument. Textual units, annotations, identifiers, relationships, and derived interfaces are documented so that readers may inspect both the evidence and the operations performed upon it.

Textual representation
Passage-level structures organise the Greek text and translation while preserving stable references for citation and analysis.
Annotation
Entities, claims, and semantic relations are connected to their textual contexts and represented with provenance.
Linked data
RDF assertions and curated external identifiers support interoperable research across people, places, concepts, and philosophical traditions.
Research interfaces
Search, graph exploration, SPARQL, and evidence-grounded question answering offer complementary modes of enquiry.

3

Annotation data and evaluation

The annotation datasets form a citable scholarly layer rather than an invisible technical substrate. They document the identification of entities and relations, their textual anchors, and the assertions used to construct the linked-data environment.

Evaluation materials include curated gold judgements, adjudications, and reproducible test resources for assessing retrieval and evidence-grounded answers. Their publication supports methodological scrutiny and makes distinctions between source text, scholarly annotation, derived data, and system output explicit.

Open research record

Source code, technical documentation, data licences, and evaluation resources are maintained in the public project repository.
